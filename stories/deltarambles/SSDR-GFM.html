# Chapter 1: Inizio Dell'Azione

--- Experimental writing! Not meant to be... coherent in it's ideas or themes. Somewhat anachronistic, I don't aim to be historical, I just aim to refine an idea of what has been brewing in my mind. Also inconspicuously bent to my interest. — caj~ | 22 September 2026 | 02:13 --- "A chi sarà sempre riservata la gloria e la gioia di osare l'impossibile?" "A NOI!" _"A NOI!"_ **_"A NOI!"_** _12 September 19XX, 12:00, Fiume, Reggenza Italiana del Carnaro_ "Fascism is a simple creature, it seeks to rejuvenate, subjugate and rule on violence." Gramscia writes on a paper as she looks over a band of men marching while waving banners of the Arditi, an elite force of the Italian army during the previous war, and the Italian flag under the Savoy dynasty. "Yet, why do I feel so.. enthralled by this.. creature?" commented Gramscia as she stands up and feels the sun pierce through the view. "Perhaps it is the way they feed on the feelings of the people, on the national independence we had long sought after since the days of the great Garibaldi..." Gramsci shakes her head. "We don't deny this, but still, it does hurt that we participated in a war that only made us bleed out in the mountains." Gramscia reflects, as she takes out of her big purse a cigarette box. "The socialist parties, even with their anti-war stance failed to take advantage of the revolutionary period..." Gramscia lights a cigarette as she picks up a note from a friend in a factory in Turin. "Comrade Gramscia! I know you have a long way off from Fiume, in that contested region between the Italians and Serbo-Croats, but the situation here has been dire, those damn traitors at the PSI and CGL are completely toothless and leaving us to be stared down by these bandits! I fear if these factory occupations don't spread out and take over like the Bolsheviks, the revolution might be defeated." — Errica M. "It seems they'll fail this one too." Gramscis sighs as she grabs her bag. She walks through the city, seeing the buildings occupied by people fighting for their nationalist beliefs, just like the workers at the factories, but whereas one is for the very salvation of Italy from further economic ruin, one was to restore Italy's glory, no matter the cost. "Vince o si muor!" _09 April 19XX, 12:00, Turin, Regno d'Italia_ _In an inconspicuous home.._ "It is such a desirable thing, for some people.." Gramscia writes. "To have an easy way of gaining power, to be able to change the country in a whim." Gramscia looks at the Italian translation of Das Kapital by Marx. "Unfortunately, this is not real life. Nor, how we should go about the changes in our society." Gramscia stands up and stretched her arms as she goes out of her work room to get a better view of the Italian afternoon. "Comrade!" Errica was resting on the couch, as she saw Gramscia come out of her room. "Oh. _Ciao._" Gramscia sits on the armchair beside the couch before Errica suddenly raised her arms in protest and reminded, "A—Aspetta! Comrade Amedea reserves that for herself!" Gramscia stands up. "Where is she, anyway?" she says, a bit exasperated. "Lasagna. You know how she is." answered Errica, as she winked. "Anyway, we're back to the factory tomorrow. Got any reports from the others?" Gramscia sits beside Errica. "Not really anything you wouldn't expect. A few scuffles between the police here and there." Gramscia looks at the ceiling. "Sometimes you wonder how you can _possibly_ set up a worker's state directly at Rome..." Errica retorts, "Ah, but comrade, once all the socialists, unions and other sympathetic workers come to our side, we should be able to force the government to fall! We even have self-defence militias!" Gramscia nods. "Do the _traditori riformisti_ count in the socialists?" she said sarcastically. "Of course not!" Errica says. "But this does not mean we can't sap their support base from them! If we can just convince the populace, gather more support..." _Knock, knock._ Errica looks at the door. "Eh, you answer that." Errica leans further back on the couch. "Couldn't be Amedea, she just barges in." she further remarked. Gramsci stands up and goes to answer the door. _The door opens._ "Mi chiamo Benita Amilcare Andrea Mussolini, conosciuta dai miei compagni come 'Accigue'." introduced Benita, as she extended her hand out. Benita was carrying a big purse of unknown papers. "I'm the leader of the Fasci d'Azione Rivoluzionara. I have a proposal but we need to be somewhere private." she added. "Oh? You traitors who supported the war?" Gramscia tilted her head. "What do you want?" she asked. "We.. want to cooperate with you." answered Benita, as Gramscia's eyes widened. "Huh? Seriously?" she raised one of her eyebrows as Benita nodded. "Sí. But not here." she says as she comes in and Errica notices and immediately scrambled to get up. "Hey! Traditore Benita!" she called out. "Ciao." Benita looks at Errica. "Why do you care? You weren't even part of—" Gramscia stepped between the two. "Comrade, she just wants to talk." she says to Errica as she rolled her eyes. "Yeah right. You better sure nothing's concrete without the factory councils knowing." Gramscis nodded. "Of course." she said, as Benita and Gramscia go to her workroom. --- "Comrade, I am thankful you accepted this.. dealing of ours." commented Benita as the two sat down on the chairs in the work room. "Don't... call me that." said Gramscia as she sighed. "I'm just curious on what you _futurists_ and.. what's it now, _fascists—?_" Gramscia waves her hand dismissively while putting on her glasses. "—are doing." Gramscia clears her throat. "You're supposedly somewhat socialist. But, in recent times, **especially** due to figures like Gabriela in Fiume, it seems you and your people are more focused on the glory of the state than the liberation of all?" Benita nods. "I do not deny that." Benita brings out a piece of paper from her bag and puts it onto the desk. "Here." Gramscia reads the paper that is of a report of the police on the factory occupations with worrying detail on connections and key leaders like Gramscia and Errica. "What.." Gramscia adjusts her glasses. "How do you..?" Gramscia questions Benita. "We raided a police station~" Benita winks. "The great Arditi fight for the people of Italy now. No more kings, bosses or landowners! Just the grand movement of patriots led by the great leaders." said Benita in a sort of speech-like way. "Patriots..?" Gramsci sets the paper down. "You're not telling me you're replacing the state with your state while keeping the nationalism?" Gramasci crosses her arms. "While I acknowledge our differences and the reasons for your group's split from the PSI.." Gramsci leans in. "We can not work with an organization that seeks to revitalize only Italy, over anyone." Gramsci raises her hand, palm up. "Such as the Serbo-Croats, the French, or the Arabs in the colonies." Benita nods. "We are not here to debate on the ideas. I have simply showed you this paper to prove that my people are on your side. The side of the oppressed, the downtrodden and the forgotten." Benita brings out one more paper. "My people have already disrupted multiple police raids and attacks, and have sustained casualties for the revolution." Benita points at the paper and is a bit more firm. "I don't say lightly that my men are dying for the socialist revolution. We are simply the vanguard that use our glorious will to suppress and wipe away the vestiges of the old." Gramscia looks at the paper, a report of suppression being interrupted by gang of black-shirted men beating police and stealing armory, and that they haven't been able to counter them. "This looks very susceptible to army counter-attacks..." Gramscia adjusts her glasses. "But I do appreciate the help. It is just a bit... harrowing to cooperate with your movement, even if it gains popularity with the fighting veterans and rowdy lumpenproletariat..." Gramscia leans back on her chair. "Give me a minute to think..." Gramscia looks at the ceiling. ---
